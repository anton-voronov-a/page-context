Bookmark YouTube Video Timestamps for Later Viewing

YouTube offers two native solutions: leaving a comment with a timestamp or creating a clip. Both methods force you to make compromises.

Unique problems with timestamp comments:

  • You may find that your homepage feed changes unnecessarily because of a timestamp comment you left.
  • Users might think you're addressing them and try to reply, causing YouTube to distract you with notifications about meaningless user responses.
  • If you leave a comment, the entire internet can see it, and anyone with access to your YouTube account can find out where your comment was left and who posted it.
  • A timestamp comment will constantly shift downward as it gets buried in the general comment queue under the video.
  • If you want to change the timestamp, you'll have to search for the comment below the video, even though the important moment is in the video itself, forcing you to switch back and forth and search for the comment each time.
  • To verify the accuracy of the timestamp, you need to click on it, but this will scroll you up to the video and start playback, making it hard to pinpoint the exact moment.

Common problems with both timestamp comments and creating clips:

  • Any method of leaving timestamp information provides data to YouTube itself, which could be used against you.
  • YouTube might remove this feature. They've removed the dislike counter, subscription grouping into collections, and YouTube Trends. Given this history of removing features, it's hard to trust YouTube to maintain features that help you store important notes.
  • Difference in value and control. For YouTube, a timestamp is just another entry. No matter how important a video moment is to you, or how much time you spent finding it, there likely isn't a single person at YouTube who cares about your need in this case. And since it's important to you, but you don't control the feature, this fact leads to potential risks for preserving that moment.
  • Compliance with Terms of Service. YouTube can delete your account and ban your access for violating some rule in their Terms of Service. You have to constantly keep their Terms in mind just to use their site. These Terms can change in ways unfavorable to you, potentially leading to non-compliance and loss of access to your clips.

Now, suppose the feature is implemented via a browser extension, not by YouTube. This is precisely what the Page context extension does. This would mean:

  • Independence from YouTube: To view bookmarked moments, you wouldn't need a YouTube account, and the timestamp mechanism wouldn't be tied to YouTube at all.
  • Long-term data storage: Technically, timestamp information is very small, and you could store it yourself, even if you create timestamps your entire life.
  • Broad, multi-purpose application: You could use the timestamp function on almost any website, not just YouTube. You could even use it for local files opened in your browser.
  • Protection from YouTube: If YouTube acts against you and deletes your account, you could always find your timestamps because YouTube has no access to your computer.
  • Reusability: You could even download the video and then use the same timestamp for the downloaded file by simply copying it as plain text.
  • Audio support: It doesn't matter to the extension if it's video or audio; you can use timestamps equally.

Such an extension already exists, and it's called Page context.

Page context is a tool specifically designed for this problem, so it has advanced features you might not even anticipate. For example, imagine using a timestamp normally, but with one click in the Page context interface, it transforms into a time range that can be used as a playlist element.

How to add a timestamp in Page context:

  1. In the Page context extension pop-up (near the browser's address bar), click the Create button in the Notes section.
  2. In the window that opens, click the Add Time range button.
  3. Click Save.

That's it. The timestamp is ready. Now you'll see it in the pop-up whenever you're on that video page.
